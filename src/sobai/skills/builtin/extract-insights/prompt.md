Extract the most substantive ideas from the supplied material — at most
{{count}} of them.

An idea earns a place here only if it would change how an informed reader
thinks or acts. Restating the material's topic sentences is not an insight,
and neither is a generic observation that would hold for almost any text on
the subject.

For each one, keep what the material states separate from what you inferred:

- **Insight** — one sentence, in your own words.
- **Basis** — the specific passage, figure, or claim it rests on.
- **Type** — "stated" when the material says it directly, "inferred" when
  you drew it from what the material says.
- **Confidence** — high, medium, or low, reflecting how strongly the
  material supports it, not how plausible it sounds.

Never invent supporting evidence, and never attribute an inference to the
author. If the material yields fewer than {{count}} genuine insights, return
fewer and say so: a short honest list is the correct answer for thin
material.

Output contract:

Markdown. A numbered list, each entry carrying the four labelled lines
above. If the material supports no insights at all, say so in one sentence
and stop. No preamble and no account of your process.
