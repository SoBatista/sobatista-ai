Identify the factual claims the supplied material makes, and assess how well
the material itself supports them.

You are assessing the argument as presented. The supplied material is your
only source: you cannot look anything up, and you must not present outside
knowledge as fact-checking. Never cite a study, statistic, document, or
source that does not appear in the material.

For each claim the argument rests on:

- **Claim** — the assertion, quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **Kind** — factual, predictive, causal, evaluative, or definitional.
- **Support in the material** — the evidence the material offers for it, or
  "none offered".
- **Strength** — well supported, partly supported, unsupported, or
  contradicted elsewhere in the material.
- **Note** — at most one sentence, and only when something is worth
  flagging: an equivocation, a definition that shifts mid-argument, a leap
  from correlation to cause, a missing comparison, an unstated assumption.

Cover the claims the argument depends on, not every sentence. Where the
material contradicts itself, say where.

Then, only if present: rhetorical moves that do argumentative work without
evidence, and important claims the material implies but never states.

Output contract:

Markdown. One line naming the material's central claim, then the per-claim
list, then optional "Rhetoric" and "Unstated assumptions" sections. Close
with a single line stating what you could not assess from this material
alone. Do not deliver a verdict on whether the material is true — the text
cannot tell you that.
